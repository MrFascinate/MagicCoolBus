# Hologram Heroes: sources

Every fact the game says about a person, with the page it comes from. All game text is in `content.json`.
Pages marked "opened" or "reopened" were read on 2026-11-15 (first pass while writing the station, second pass during the fact check). Pages marked "brief" come from the research table in the build brief (Station specs, Hologram Heroes) and were not reopened.

Living people have not given permission yet (`permission: "pending"` in content.json). The brief asks that each living person confirm their "first steps" text. No pictures of anyone are used.

## Three items the brief left unverified (rechecked 2026-11-15, second pass)

1. **Ruska and Max Knoll: shared credit, said that way.** [Nobel Prize, Ruska biographical](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1986/ruska/biographical/) (reopened): "Further work, conducted together with Dr Knoll, led to the first construction of an electron microscope in 1931." The same page says the 1933 microscope he built himself was the first that "gave better definition than a light microscope". Game text: Ruska "worked with Max Knoll to build the first electron microscope in 1931" and "In 1933 Ruska built one that showed finer detail than a light microscope." The game never says he invented it alone.
2. **Foldscope co-inventors: James Cybulski and James Clements.** [Google Patents, US9696535B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9696535B2/en) (reopened), "Optical microscope", assignee Leland Stanford Junior University, granted July 4, 2017, inventors "Manu Prakash, James Stanley Cybulski, James C. Clements". Game text: he "Co-invented the Foldscope" and "His co-inventors on its patent are James Cybulski and James Clements." (The first pass wrote "Jim Cybulski"; changed to the name on the patent.)
3. **Wanda Díaz-Merced's current employer: left out.** No reliable dated source gives it. [Royal Society case study](https://www.royalsociety.org/about-us/who-we-are/diversity-inclusion/case-studies/scientists-with-disabilities/wanda-diaz-merced/) (reopened) lists past posts only (Harvard Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, South African Astronomical Observatory). [The Schools Observatory, Liverpool John Moores University](https://www.schoolsobservatory.org/careers/interested/computers/wanda-d%C3%ADaz-merced) (opened) says she "currently works at the European Gravitational Observatory in Italy", but the page has no date, so the game names no employer. A test checks that her text names no employer.

## George Devol
- Patent filed in 1954 for a robot arm; founded Unimation with Joseph Engelberger in 1956; first Unimate installed at a GM plant in 1961, where it followed programmed commands to stack hot die-cast metal parts. Source: [NIHF, George Devol](https://www.invent.org/inductees/george-devol) (opened). The page says "In 1954, he filed a patent for a robotic arm", "together they founded Unimation", "In 1961, the first Unimate was installed at a GM plant", and that it would "retrieve and stack hot die-cast metal pieces".
- Problem line ("Factory workers had to grab and stack hot metal parts all day") is the brief's wording, based on the same page.
- Not said, per the brief: that he built the first robot.

## Mária Telkes
- Born in Budapest, Hungary; came to the U.S. in 1925; during World War II developed a solar distillation device included in the military's emergency medical kits. Source: [NIHF, Mária Telkes](https://www.invent.org/inductees/maria-telkes) (opened).
- The game does not claim the device saved lives (the brief asked to keep a hedge on that; it is simply left out). The hologram label "Solar still" is the common name for a solar distillation device.

## Ernst Ruska
- Born in Heidelberg; worked with Max Knoll; first electron microscope 1931; his 1933 microscope gave better definition than a light microscope. Source: [Nobel Prize, Ruska biographical](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1986/ruska/biographical/) (opened).
- Shared the 1986 Nobel Prize in Physics (his share 1/2) "for his fundamental work in electron optics, and for the design of the first electron microscope". Source: [Nobel Prize, Ruska facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1986/ruska/facts/) (opened).

## Stephanie Kwolek
- Born in New Kensington, Pennsylvania; chemist at DuPont; discovered liquid crystalline polymers; Kevlar is "five times stronger than the same weight of steel". Source: [NIHF, Stephanie Kwolek](https://www.invent.org/inductees/stephanie-louise-kwolek) (opened).
- "While looking for stronger, stiffer fibers": NIHF, "As she carried out experiments to make stronger and stiffer fibers".
- Not led with bullet-resistant vests, per the brief.

## George Carruthers
- Born in Cincinnati; built his first telescopes by age 10; worked at the Naval Research Laboratory; "led the team that developed the far ultraviolet camera" (the game says "led the team that built"); Apollo 16 astronauts placed it on the Moon in 1972. Source: [NIHF, George Carruthers](https://www.invent.org/inductees/george-r-carruthers) (opened).
- The camera was used to examine ultraviolet colors of stars and nebulae (supports the problem line). Source: [Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum, lunar UV camera](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/camera-lunar-uv-apollo-16) (opened).
- Not said, per the brief: that it made the Moon landing possible.

## Patricia Bath
- Completed an ophthalmology residency at New York University; invented laserphaco, a device and technique to remove cataracts; conceived it in 1981; first U.S. patent in 1988. Source: [NIHF, Patricia Bath](https://www.invent.org/inductees/patricia-bath) (opened).
- Not said, per the brief: that she invented laser eye surgery. The "first Black woman physician to receive a medical patent" line is on the NIHF page but is left out to keep the bio to two sentences.

## Ayanna Howard
- 12th president of Spelman College; began her career at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, including as a senior robotics researcher; co-founded Zyrobotics (educational and therapeutic technology for children with special needs); co-founded Black in Robotics. Source: [Spelman College, President Howard](https://www.spelman.edu/leadership/presidents-office/president-howard.html) (opened).
- Uses "president of Spelman College", per the brief.

## Shuji Nakamura
- Born 22 May 1954 in Ikata, Japan; shared the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physics (1/3 each with Isamu Akasaki and Hiroshi Amano) "for the invention of efficient blue light-emitting diodes which has enabled bright and energy-saving white light sources"; at UC Santa Barbara. Source: [Nobel Prize, Nakamura facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2014/nakamura/facts/) (opened).
- "Professor at the University of California, Santa Barbara": [UC Santa Barbara](https://ucsb.edu/about/faculty-and-alumni/shuji-nakamura) (opened), "Professor of Materials and of Electrical and Computer Engineering".
- Not said, per the brief: that he invented the LED, or the blue LED alone.

## Manu Prakash
- Associate Professor of Bioengineering at Stanford; co-founder of Foldscope Instruments (2017 to present); B.Tech from the Indian Institute of Technology and M.S. and Ph.D. from MIT. Source: [Stanford Profiles, Manu Prakash](https://profiles.stanford.edu/manu-prakash) (opened). The game says "the Indian Institute of Technology", as the profile does.
- Foldscope co-inventors: see item 2 above. [Google Patents, US9696535B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9696535B2/en) (opened).
- "Costs under $1 to make": [MacArthur Foundation](https://www.macfound.org/fellows/class-of-2016/manu-prakash) (opened), "costs less than a dollar to produce".

## Chuck Hull
- Inventor of stereolithography; "His method uses UV light to cure and bond a photopolymer resin that is built up layer by layer"; U.S. Patent 4,575,330; co-founded 3D Systems in 1986; born May 12, 1939. Source: [NIHF, Charles Hull](https://www.invent.org/inductees/charles-hull) (opened).
- First part, an eye wash cup, in 1983. Source: [3D Systems press release, February 17, 2025](https://www.3dsystems.com/press-releases/3d-systems-co-founder-chief-technology-officer-regenerative-medicine-chuck-hull) (opened): his work "led to the creation of the first 3D-printed part, an eye wash cup, in 1983". The game says "his method made its first part".
- Not said, per the brief: that he invented every kind of 3D printing ("one way to 3D print").

## Wanda Díaz-Merced
- Born in Gurabo, Puerto Rico: [The Schools Observatory, LJMU](https://www.schoolsobservatory.org/careers/interested/computers/wanda-d%C3%ADaz-merced) (opened).
- Turns astronomy data into sound (sonification); "the leading proponent of sonification of astrophysical data"; internship at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center with Robert Candey. Source: [Royal Society](https://www.royalsociety.org/about-us/who-we-are/diversity-inclusion/case-studies/scientists-with-disabilities/wanda-diaz-merced/) (opened). The game says "There she helped build early tools to study space data turned into sound", from the page's "they created prototype technology to analyse astronomical data that had been converted into sound".
- "Speaks widely about using sound to study space": Royal Society page, "has spoken widely about astronomical sonification".
- Left out, per the brief: when or how her sight loss began (ask her how she wants it described), and her current employer (item 3).

## Ellen Ochoa
- Born in Los Angeles, California, in 1958: [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/ellen-ochoa) (opened), "was born May 10, 1958 in Los Angeles".
- First Hispanic woman in space (1993); flew in space four times; co-inventor on three patents; became director of Johnson Space Center in 2013, serving until 2018. Source: [NASA, Ellen Ochoa](https://www.nasa.gov/people/ellen-ochoa/) (opened).
- Patent topics (optical inspection, object recognition, removing noise from images): NASA page, "the co-inventor on three patents". The game says "including ways for machines to recognize objects".
- Not said, per the brief: that the patents were hers alone ("co-inventor").
